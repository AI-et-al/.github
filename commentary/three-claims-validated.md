# If all three hold: a commentary on OpenAI's October 26, 2026 announcement

*Premise, as requested: assume each of the three claimed results is validated in turn, and ask what follows for science, AI, civilization and history. Epistemic tags are used throughout: **[Fact]** for things read directly from primary artifacts, **[Reported]** for secondary coverage of statements I could not read at the source, **[Interpretation]**, **[Hypothesis]** and **[Speculation]** for my own reasoning at decreasing levels of support. Consensus levels are noted where a field has one.*

*Source note. The announcement page on openai.com and most news sites are unreachable from this environment. What follows is reconstructed from OpenAI's public `openai/math` repository on GitHub (manuscript catalogue, history file, Lean formalization catalogue and the two new Lean "comparator" files), from search-engine summaries of coverage (Gizmodo, Al Jazeera, Scientific American, TechCrunch, The Next Web and others), and from a search snippet of Greg Brockman's post. Where the sources disagree on a date or number I say so.*

---

## 0. What was actually announced

**[Reported]** On October 26, 2026 OpenAI published a post titled "Working with academic communities to build scientific understanding." Its opening line, as quoted in coverage: "We are announcing three new discoveries in mathematics, computer science, and physics about harmonic analysis, complexity theory, and quantum field theory." Greg Brockman's accompanying post read: "Announcing three major scientific discoveries, one each in mathematics (harmonic analysis), computer science (complexity theory), and physics (quantum field theory). AI starting to help us improve our understanding of the universe!" Dan Roberts of OpenAI posted a thread describing the computer-science collaboration as one that "will understand and explain a result concerning the Exponential Time Hypothesis."

**[Fact]** Two of the three results are public. The `openai/math` repository's history file for October 26 lists two new manuscripts, both dated October 25, 2026, both with Lean formalizations:

1. *Local smoothing for the Euclidean wave equation.* The history entry says it "uses an auxiliary PDE to establish Sogge's local smoothing in every spatial dimension of at least two" and that it "also yields the Kakeya maximal and dimension conjectures and Stein's spherical restriction estimates in the interior of the conjectured range."
2. *The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False* (catalogue family 378). The entry describes "a deterministic algorithm that exactly counts satisfying assignments of fixed-width CNF formulas in subexponential time and polynomial space," adds "The case of 3-SAT refutes the Exponential Time Hypothesis," and notes that an independent group led by Russell Impagliazzo, Ramamohan Paturi and Ryan Williams will examine the proof and that the materials were made public at their request.

**[Fact]** The Lean side is where "validated" acquires a precise meaning, so here is what is actually certified. The local-smoothing comparator file states six theorems, none containing `sorry`, `axiom` or `opaque` in the file itself (the modules they import were not inspected):

- `euclidean_local_smoothing`: for n ≥ 2 and p ≥ 2, the half-wave propagator on the slab R^n × [1,2] is bounded in L^p from the Bessel-potential space of order max(0, (n−1)/2 − n/p) + ε, with zero loss at p = 2. That is Sogge's 1991 conjecture, in its full range.
- `bochner_riesz`: uniform L^p bounds for Bochner–Riesz means for every p in [1, ∞] and every δ above the conjectured critical index n|1/p − 1/2| − 1/2. That is the Bochner–Riesz conjecture.
- `spherical_restriction`: the extension estimate from L^a of the sphere to L^b of R^n throughout the strict conjectured range. That is Stein's restriction conjecture.
- `kakeya_maximal`: the Kakeya maximal function bound on L^n with δ^(−ε) loss, uniformly in scale.
- `kakeya`: every set containing a unit segment in every direction has Hausdorff dimension n, with no measurability assumed. That is the Kakeya set conjecture.
- `stein_square`: Stein's square-function estimate throughout the conjectured range.

**[Fact]** The complexity result's Lean documentation states: for each fixed k ≥ 2, a single deterministic multitape "primitive machine" exactly counts the satisfying assignments of a canonically encoded k-CNF on n variables in time (L+1)^{O_k(1)} · 2^{O_k(n (log log n)²/√log n)}, where L is the bit length of the encoding, while visiting only polynomially many tape cells. The manuscript claims a slightly sharper exponent, O_k(n log log n/√log n). The final Lean file contains no `sorry`, `axiom` or `opaque` and ends with `#print axioms` commands.

**[Reported]** The third result has no public artifact. Coverage describes a claimed rigorous construction of four-dimensional Yang–Mills theory and of QCD (gauge theory with fermions), plus "completions of the Standard Model," with company-reported dates of September 9 (Yang–Mills draft), September 18 (QCD draft) and October 16 (a Lean formalization of Yang–Mills existence). Multiple outlets say the claim is existence only, not the mass gap. A group led by David Gross, with Michael Douglas, Arthur Jaffe, Igor Klebanov and Nikita Nekrasov, holds the materials and controls release.

**[Reported]** The process is the novel part of the post. OpenAI describes the manuscripts as "raw research data," pairs them with outside experts it calls "shepherds" who organize review groups, says it "does not seek academic credit," says the shepherds' "conclusions are their own" and that they "do not speak on behalf of OpenAI," and concedes that "choosing initial shepherds creates asymmetry of information." The ETH group published an open letter: "Our involvement should not be taken as certification of the claim. A result of this significance requires careful, independent scrutiny." Williams added that the group signed no non-disclosure agreements, is unpaid, and that OpenAI agreed to release raw data and fund a conference. On practical consequences of the ETH refutation, OpenAI itself wrote that they "will require significant further research."

**[Fact]** Context the post sits inside. The same repository holds 721 manuscripts in 373 result families released on October 6 from an unnamed internal model, with roughly 42% of top-line results formalized. Its README says most results came from one procedure averaging about three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking compute, over about 4,000 posed problems. The October 7 history entry records three manuscripts withdrawn after "a sign error invalidated a stabilization-trace cancellation argument," plus fixes to fourteen others. **[Reported]** Before that: a September 8 Navier–Stokes claim (a forced blow-up construction, 166 pages plus Lean, built with a swarm of about 10,000 agents, now under review by the Clay Institute and the subject of a priority dispute); a September 11 declaration by 25 or more Fields Medalists titled "A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics"; and the September 21 formation of an Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence at the IAS (members include Gowers, Witten and Hairer), whose first request to OpenAI was to stop testing open problems on proprietary models, a request not followed.

So: "validated" will mean three different things. For the two public results it means a review group confirms that the Lean definitions match the classical statements and that nothing hidden lives in the imported modules or the machine model. For the physics result it means the Gross group says so, with artifacts nobody else has seen. Keep that asymmetry in mind through everything below.

## 1. Claim one validated: the wave stops the needle

**[Fact, consensus]** Harmonic analysis has for fifty years organized itself around a tower of conjectures, each implying the next: local smoothing at the top, then Bochner–Riesz, then restriction, then the Kakeya maximal conjecture, then the Kakeya set conjecture at the bottom. Tao's 2001 Notices article "From rotating needles to stability of waves" is the standard map. Progress has been by dimension and by inches: Kakeya in the plane (Davies 1971), restriction and Bochner–Riesz in the plane (Fefferman, Carleson–Sjölin, early 1970s), Wolff's (n+2)/2 Kakeya bound (1995), the polynomial method (Dvir 2008 in finite fields, Guth after), local smoothing in 2+1 dimensions (Guth–Wang–Zhang 2020), Kakeya in three dimensions (Wang–Zahl 2025). Each step was a career-defining result.

**[Interpretation]** The claimed manuscript sits at the top of the tower and the Lean file certifies the entire tower at once, in every dimension. If it validates, a single October afternoon closes a program begun with Kakeya's 1917 question about turning a needle and Besicovitch's 1919 construction. There is no precedent in modern analysis for one document resolving six named conjectures of this stature. The nearest comparisons are not within analysis: Perelman's three preprints (2002–03) resolving geometrization and Poincaré, or Wiles (1994) closing Fermat with Taniyama–Shimura.

**[Interpretation]** The downstream reach is wide because these estimates are tools, not endpoints. Restriction and Bochner–Riesz feed into the convergence theory of Fourier integrals in L^p, into Strichartz-type estimates for dispersive equations, into spectral theory of the Laplacian, and through decoupling (Bourgain–Demeter 2015) into analytic number theory. Local smoothing in all dimensions would sharpen what is known about focusing of waves, with consequences for low-regularity well-posedness of nonlinear wave equations. Many papers that currently say "assuming the restriction conjecture" or "in dimension two" would become unconditional overnight.

**[Hypothesis]** The phrase "auxiliary PDE" in the history entry is the most interesting technical detail available. The human route to local smoothing ran through square functions, decoupling and incidence geometry of tubes; a proof through an auxiliary partial differential equation would be a different mechanism, more in the tradition of Bourgain's and Tao's use of the wave equation itself as the organizing object. If validated, mathematics acquires not only a theorem but a method, and the method is the larger prize. Tao's reported reaction to the October 6 batch applies here with more force: the proofs may contain "clever new ideas that will be fruitful once digested."

**[Interpretation]** And here is the first loss, already visible in the Fields Medalists' declaration: the field's sociology is built on the assumption that understanding and proving are the same activity. "Solving problems is only a tool and proxy for achieving the primary goal of conceptual understanding and insight," the declaration says. A validated proof nobody understands is a new object in the culture of mathematics. We have precedents, and they are instructive: the Four Color Theorem (Appel–Haken 1976) was accepted and resented for two decades until Robertson–Sanders–Seymour–Thomas made it comprehensible; the Classification of Finite Simple Groups took a generation to consolidate into a readable second-generation proof; Hales's Kepler proof was fully trusted only after the eleven-year Flyspeck formalization. In each case the field built a decade-long program of digestion. **[Hypothesis]** Expect the same here: the next ten years of harmonic analysis would be, in large part, the human reconstruction of a machine's argument, and the first human to give a conceptual account of why the auxiliary PDE works would deserve a prize of the kind the machine cannot receive.

**[Fact]** One caveat that validation itself must clear: the six theorems are stated in OpenAI's own Lean library (`OAI.Analysis.Fourier.*`, `OAI.MeasureTheory.Kakeya`), using its own definitions of the Fourier transform normalization, the half-wave integral, the Bessel potential and the Kakeya set. A Lean kernel check certifies that these formal statements follow from the library's axioms. It does not certify that `halfWaveIntegral` is the half-wave propagator a human means. The repository ships a "Comparator" tool, apparently so that reviewers can state the theorem independently and have the two formal statements compared. This definitional audit is the real work of the review group, and it is tractable: these definitions are a few hundred lines, not a few hundred pages.

## 2. Claim two validated: the hub hypothesis falls

**[Fact, consensus]** The Exponential Time Hypothesis (Impagliazzo–Paturi 2001) says there is a constant c > 0 such that 3-SAT on n variables requires 2^{cn} time. Its stronger sibling SETH says the constant for k-SAT approaches 1 as k grows. ETH implies P ≠ NP but is much stronger. Over twenty-five years it became the hub of two research programs: parameterized complexity, where it yields tight lower bounds such as no f(k)·n^{o(k)} algorithm for k-Clique and no 2^{o(√n)} algorithms for planar problems, and fine-grained complexity, where SETH underwrites the n^{2−ε} barriers for edit distance, longest common subsequence, orthogonal vectors and dozens more. The 2011 Lokshtanov–Marx–Saurabh survey already listed consequences across graph theory, computational geometry, databases and bioinformatics; the count is now in the hundreds of papers.

**[Fact]** The claimed algorithm counts, exactly and deterministically, the satisfying assignments of any k-CNF for fixed k, in time 2^{O(n (log log n)²/√log n)} times a polynomial in the input length, using polynomial space. Counting is strictly harder than deciding, so this refutes not only ETH but the counting version #ETH, and since #2-SAT is already #P-complete (Valiant 1979), even the k = 2 case is a result in its own right.

**[Interpretation]** The first thing to say clearly, because the headlines will not: this would not touch P versus NP, and it would not make SAT easy. The exponent n/√log n is 2^{o(n)}, so ETH dies, but it is enormously larger than n^{0.99}, so 3-SAT would not be placed in 2^{n^ε} time for any small ε, and the class SUBEXP would be as far away as before. In numbers: at n = 1000 the exponent saving is a factor of about √10 ≈ 3, before the (log log n)² term and the hidden constants eat it. OpenAI's own line that practical use "will require significant further research" is, if anything, understated. The right analogies are the AKS primality test (2002), deterministic and polynomial and never used, and Babai's quasipolynomial graph isomorphism (2015): landmarks that reorganized a conceptual map while leaving the engineering map untouched.

**[Interpretation]** What does change is the map. SETH dies with ETH, so every conditional lower bound of the form "if SETH then no n^{2−ε} algorithm for X" loses its support. The theorems remain true as implications; they simply say nothing. Fine-grained complexity would have to rebuild on its surviving pillars (the 3SUM and APSP hypotheses, which are independent of SETH) or on new ones. Parameterized complexity keeps W[1] ≠ FPT as an open belief but loses its favorite quantitative hammer. **[Hypothesis]** More unsettling is the epistemic lesson. ETH was the hypothesis theorists treated as nearly as safe as P ≠ NP; SETH was the one people were willing to doubt. Williams's own 2018 essay on estimated likelihoods put the two apart for exactly this reason. If the "safe" one is false, the field's calibration about hardness was off, and the empirical basis for believing hardness conjectures ("many strong people tried for 25 years") turns out to measure the human search process, not the problem.

**[Interpretation]** The second thing to say: this is the cleanest of the three claims to validate, and the cleanest demonstration of what formal verification buys. An algorithm, a correctness proof and a running-time bound are all stated inside one machine model and checked by one kernel. The remaining audit is finite and specific: confirm that the "primitive machine" cannot do anything a Turing machine cannot do in a step; confirm that the canonical encoding length L is polynomial in n and m, since the (L+1)^{O_k(1)} factor would hide exponential time if the encoding were pathological; confirm that `SAT.count` is the standard count. The ETH group can do this in weeks. If they confirm, "ETH is false" becomes the first major result in complexity theory whose entire proof was never read by a human before being believed.

**[Hypothesis]** On the "algorithms to lower bounds" front: Williams's program (2010 onward) shows that sufficiently fast satisfiability algorithms for circuit classes translate into circuit lower bounds, and that is how NEXP ⊄ ACC⁰ was proved. Whether a subexponential #k-SAT algorithm feeds that pipeline depends on how it behaves on richer circuit classes, which no public source describes. It is the first question a complexity theorist will ask.

**[Speculation, civilizational]** The refutation is also a warning shot for cryptography, even though it threatens no deployed scheme. Modern cryptography rests on hardness beliefs of exactly this type: that lattice problems, factoring and discrete logarithms have no fast algorithms because nobody has found one. The NIST post-quantum standards finalized in 2024 lean on lattice assumptions. ETH was a cousin of those beliefs, held with comparable confidence by comparable experts, and if a machine refutes it in a week of October, the prior on "a machine finds a better lattice algorithm" has to move. Not because the two problems are related mathematically, but because the thing that made us confident, the failure of human search, has stopped being informative.

## 3. Claim three validated: the Standard Model exists

**[Fact, consensus]** Quantum field theory is the most precisely confirmed framework in science (the electron's anomalous magnetic moment agrees with experiment to about ten significant figures), and no interacting quantum field theory in four spacetime dimensions has ever been shown to exist as a mathematical object. Constructive field theory succeeded in two dimensions (φ⁴, Yang–Mills, Gross–Neveu) and three (φ⁴, by Glimm and Jaffe in 1973), then stalled in four. Balaban's 1980s results on ultraviolet stability for lattice Yang–Mills were the high-water mark. Aizenman and Duminil-Copin proved in 2021 that four-dimensional φ⁴ is trivial in the continuum limit, which is why the Higgs sector by itself is not expected to define a continuum theory and why the Clay problem (Jaffe–Witten 2000) asks for Yang–Mills, which is asymptotically free, and asks for two things: existence satisfying axioms at least as strong as Wightman's, and a mass gap Δ > 0.

**[Interpretation]** If the Gross group validates existence of four-dimensional Yang–Mills and QCD, physics pays a seventy-year IOU. The strong interaction, computed numerically on lattices for forty years (light hadron masses within a few percent, Dürr et al. 2008), would for the first time be known to have the continuum limit those computations assume. The history here is a pattern, and the pattern is the point: Newton's calculus ran 150 years before Cauchy and Weierstrass; Dirac's delta function waited two decades for Schwartz's distributions; Feynman's path integral still lacks a measure in four dimensions. Physics has repeatedly been right before it was rigorous. Hilbert's sixth problem, the axiomatization of physics, would move from aspiration to partial fact.

**[Interpretation]** The phrase "completions of the Standard Model" deserves a careful reading. As a mathematical claim it presumably means constructing an asymptotically free gauge theory with fermions (and possibly scalars) into which the Standard Model's low-energy content embeds, thereby evading the triviality of the abelian and Higgs sectors. It is not a claim about which theory nature uses above the electroweak scale. If validated, it would nonetheless be the first time the matter content of our universe had been shown to be describable by a mathematically existing theory. Wigner's "unreasonable effectiveness" would gain an odd coda: we built the physics first, and the mathematics was delivered fifty years later by a machine.

**[Interpretation]** What remains open matters as much. Existence without a mass gap is a theory that is known to make sense but not known to have the single most important qualitative property of the real strong force: that its excitations are massive, that there are no long-range strong forces, that quarks are confined. The second half of the Clay problem would still be the second half.

**[Fact]** On the Lean claim: a formalization of the existence of a four-dimensional quantum field theory would be, by a wide margin, the largest formalization in history. It would require measure theory on infinite-dimensional spaces, reflection positivity, the Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction theorem and renormalization-group estimates, almost none of which exist in Mathlib. If the October 16 formalization is real, the formal library that came with it would itself be a scientific resource of the first importance, and a strong argument for the review group to release it.

**[Interpretation]** And the governance novelty is sharpest here. The artifacts are held not by OpenAI but by five senior physicists who are, in effect, running a private embargo. Mathematics has one good precedent for community verification of a raw manuscript: Perelman, whose arXiv postings were checked over three years by three independent teams (Kleiner–Lott, Cao–Zhu, Morgan–Tian). It also has a cautionary one: Mochizuki's inter-universal Teichmüller theory, "published" in 2021 by a journal at his own institute while the field's consensus remained that the proof had a gap. The shepherd model is closer to Perelman than to Mochizuki, with one difference that cuts both ways: Perelman could be asked questions and mostly declined; the model can be asked questions, but only by OpenAI.

## 4. The trio together: what it would mean for AI

**[Interpretation]** Take the three as a set and the pattern is unmistakable. Each sits at the top of an implication chain or at the center of a hub, so that one result cascades into many. A tweet from the author of the ProofAtlas list noted that the announcement would resolve six entries of its top 500 open problems. **[Hypothesis]** This is a selection signature. The model is not wandering the mathematical landscape; it is being aimed by people at the targets with the largest downstream footprint. That matters for interpretation in two directions. It means the announcement is also a demonstration designed to be maximally legible to outsiders. It also means the model's actual capability is at least as broad as the targets chosen, since you cannot aim at what you cannot hit.

**[Fact]** The cadence is the other headline. Company-reported dates put the Yang–Mills draft on September 9, QCD on September 18, the ETH disproof on October 16 with Lean the next day, local smoothing on October 18 with Lean the next day. Formalizing a result of this scale in a day is itself unprecedented: Flyspeck took eleven years, the Liquid Tensor Experiment a year and a half for part of one theorem. Whatever else is true, the bottleneck has moved. Generating proofs and formalizing them are both now fast; reading, understanding and adjudicating are slow. A recent arXiv title names the regime exactly: "Verification abundance, adjudication scarcity."

**[Interpretation]** On capability, the trajectory is now short enough to state in one breath. IMO gold-medal performance in July 2025; AI-assisted resolutions of Erdős problems from late 2025, after an embarrassing false start that October when claimed "solutions" turned out to be literature lookups; an arXiv paper in July 2026 whose title claims "autonomous disproofs of the sum-product conjecture over R" (I could read only its title); 721 manuscripts on open problems in October 2026, including claims as large as a zero-free region for the zeta function; and now three Millennium-class results in the same month. Fifteen months from olympiad to the top of several fields. If the three validate, the question "can AI do research mathematics" is closed, and the live questions become which mathematics, chosen by whom, and verified how.

**[Hypothesis]** The three results also mark the boundary of the method. All three live where a proof can be stated formally and checked by a kernel; even the physics claim is a theorem of constructive analysis. The shepherd model works because the artifact can be independently checked. The same model will be copied for AI-generated claims in biology, chemistry and medicine within a year or two, and there it will struggle, because the wet-lab equivalent of a Lean kernel does not exist. The next civilizational bottleneck is not AI reasoning but replication capacity in the empirical sciences.

**[Interpretation]** On openness: the manuscripts and Lean code are public under Apache-2.0, which is more than most human mathematicians provide. The model, the prompts and (for all but ten of 719 manuscripts) the reasoning traces are not. This is a new shape: open outputs, closed process. Anyone can check the theorem; nobody can reproduce the discovery. The IAS group's reported statement that AI can now produce arguments "without the person who prompted it being able to understand, verify or take responsibility for them" is the correct worry, and the Lean artifacts answer only the middle word.

## 5. Civilization and history: the wild part, as requested

**[Speculation]** *The Hilbert moment, inverted.* In 1900 Hilbert's 23 problems directed a century of human effort. In 2026 the open-problem list stops being a mountain range and becomes a backlog. The status of "famous open problem" would change meaning within a decade, from a lifetime's worth of work to a weekend's worth of compute plus a year of human verification. The cultural institution that organized mathematics since Hilbert, the problem as a summons to a career, would not survive in its current form.

**[Speculation]** *Printing-press economics.* The seventeenth century produced the first complaints that there were too many books to read; the scientific journal (1665), the review and eventually peer review were the institutions that converted abundance of text into scarcity of judgment. The shepherd model is the first draft of a post-journal institution for the same problem: an embargoed raw manuscript, named adjudicators who are unpaid and un-NDA'd, formal certificates alongside prose, and a company that forswears credit. Whether it becomes the norm depends on one variable: whether the shepherds are ever embarrassed. One retracted "validated" result would end it.

**[Speculation]** *Credit and the Fields Medal.* The medal goes to humans under forty; the Clay prizes go to humans after two years of acceptance; OpenAI says it wants neither credit nor the Navier–Stokes million. The Buckmaster–Alpöge priority dispute over the Navier–Stokes work shows what happens when a prize-shaped culture meets an actor outside the prize system. **[Hypothesis]** Expect the labor of mathematicians to shift toward four activities the machine does not yet do for us: choosing problems, judging proofs, explaining them, and writing the definitions against which formal statements are compared. The last is more important than it sounds. In a world where the kernel checks the proof, the definition is where the trust lives.

**[Speculation]** *Concentration.* Three results of this magnitude from one company's unreleased model is a concentration of epistemic capability without precedent in the history of science; even the Manhattan Project published its physics eventually. The physics artifacts are withheld by independent scientists for good reasons, but the decision architecture is the point: what is released, when, and to whom is now a governance question, and the capacity to find an algorithm that breaks something could as easily be kept private as announced. Prediction markets reportedly moved twenty points on Yang–Mills within hours. Markets pricing the output of a private theorem-prover is a small sign of a large shift.

**[Speculation]** *Meaning.* Hardy's Apology promised mathematicians a kind of immortality through theorems that would outlast empires. A headline after the October 6 release asked "how much beauty have we lost?" The honest answer is that nothing true was lost and something human was displaced. If the trio validates, mathematics becomes, for most people who love it, what Latin became after it stopped being the language of science: a culture to be understood rather than a frontier to be pushed. That is not nothing. Most of what is valuable about mathematics was always in the understanding. It is also, concretely, why the three games that accompany this commentary exist: the appropriate human response to a machine that proves the theorems is to go and finally learn what they say.

**[Speculation]** *History's verdict.* If this holds, October 2026 will be remembered the way 1905 is remembered in physics or 1953 in biology: a year when a frontier moved by an amount previously measured in decades. The difference is that 1905 and 1953 had names attached. This one has a company, an unnamed model, and five physicists holding a sealed envelope.

## 6. Where I would bet against, and why

The premise of this piece was to assume validation. A critical analyst should still say where the premise is weak. Subjective probabilities, mine, stated to be argued with:

**Base rates first [Fact].** OpenAI's own repository recorded three withdrawals the day after its October 6 release, over a sign error, and 58% of that release remains unformalized. The September Navier–Stokes claim turned out to concern a forced formulation narrower than the Clay problem, and the "Spanish program" priority story was overblown in the other direction. The track record is "largely real, often narrower than the headline."

**Local smoothing [Hypothesis].** Outright falsity is unlikely because the six statements are formal and strong. The live risk is definitional mismatch (normalizations, the meaning of `halfWaveIntegral`, the exponent ranges), and the imported modules were not inspected. I would put about 75–85% on the review group confirming the main theorem as stated within a year, and somewhat less on every one of the six corollaries surviving at the claimed ranges.

**ETH [Hypothesis].** Also formal, also strong. The risks are the machine model and the encoding length. About 75–85% that the group confirms "3-SAT in 2^{o(n)} time." Note that even their confirmation would be read by many theorists as "ETH false in a model we accept," followed by a search for whether the model hides a trick; that search is healthy and should be expected to take a year.

**Yang–Mills and QCD [Hypothesis].** No artifacts, no public Lean, a seventy-year history of failure by excellent people, and the ambitious phrase "completions of the Standard Model." I would put roughly 30–45% on the Gross group endorsing the existence of four-dimensional pure Yang–Mills in the Wightman or Osterwalder–Schrader sense within a year, lower for QCD with fermions, and I would weight this one far less than the other two in any conclusion about "what has been achieved." The claim's structure also invites the Navier–Stokes failure mode: a construction that is rigorous and impressive and not quite the object the Clay statement names.

**The strongest argument against the whole exercise [Interpretation].** Validation is not binary and not an event. Perelman's proof was "accepted" in 2006 and understood over a decade; the Four Color Theorem was "proved" in 1976 and trusted in the 1990s. "Assume validated" smuggles in the assumption that the review groups' endorsement is the end of the story. In practice it would be the beginning of a decade in which the field decides how much of its own understanding it is willing to trade for a kernel's certificate.

**And the counter-counterargument [Interpretation].** Nothing above requires the results to be wrong for the institutional changes to happen. The shepherd model, the shift of mathematical labor toward adjudication, the collapse of "nobody has found an algorithm" as evidence, the pressure on proprietary models: all of these follow from the plausible possibility of the trio, which the two public Lean files have already established. The civilization-scale consequences do not wait for the Gross group.
